# Copywriting

A beautiful deck does not sell a weak argument. This file covers how to find the argument, how to phrase it, and how to check it. The frameworks here fill the five parts of [01-framework.md](01-framework.md). They never replace them.

## Research

Before writing a line, collect this. Reviews, sales calls, forums, comments and competitor pages are the best sources. Copy phrases exactly.

- **Who:** the buyer in three lines. Attitudes, what they are proud of, what embarrasses them.
- **Pains:** five, in their words.
- **Dream outcome:** in their words. The most useful line is often about freedom or status.
- **Beliefs and objections:** all of them. Each one becomes a FAQ loop.
- **What they tried:** every existing solution, and why it failed them.
- **Who they blame:** outside forces, the common enemy.
- **What is changing:** a dated, sourced fact about their market.

These questions adapt Stefan Georgi's RMBC research method. Keep at least two exact verbatims for the script, and list them in `brand.json`.

### Awareness sets the hook (Eugene Schwartz)

| Level | The buyer | Open with |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unaware | Does not see a problem | A story or a surprising fact about their world |
| Problem aware | Feels it, knows no fix | The pain, sharper than they have said it |
| Solution aware | Knows the category | Why the usual solutions fall short |
| Product aware | Knows you | What makes you different, proof |
| Most aware | Ready | The offer and the deadline |

### Sophistication sets the angle (Schwartz)

| Level | The market | Angle |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | First of its kind | Say what it does |
| 2 | A few competitors | Bigger, faster |
| 3 | Claims are tired | A named mechanism that explains how |
| 4 | Mechanisms are tired | A sharper niche or a direct comparison |
| 5 | Everyone is cynical | Identity, story, extreme specificity |

Most service markets sit at 3 or above, which is why part 2 exists.

### Two dials decide the sale

People act when pain and trust are both high. Pain rises through the problem, what it costs, and what happens if nothing changes. Trust rises through the offer, the person and the other clients. When a script feels flat, find which dial is low.

### Value equation (Alex Hormozi)

Value = (dream outcome × perceived likelihood) ÷ (time delay × effort and sacrifice). Use it on the offer loop. Raise the top with results and proof. Shrink the bottom by stating how fast it starts and how little the client must do.

## Strategy, in seven sentences

Write these in `BRIEF.md` and do not move on until each one is sharp.

1. **Hook.** A question the viewer cannot answer comfortably.
2. **Big idea.** One line that reframes the problem. The strongest ones contrast two units or two worlds: "reputation is counted in years, a system is counted in weeks".
3. **Promise.** [Precise result] in [delay], without [pain], even if [objection].
4. **Picture.** One physical moment after the result.
5. **Turning point.** What the buyer does now that changes everything, in plain words.
6. **Mechanism.** A two-word name, an image anyone can draw, three pillars that begin the same way.
7. **Single action.** What they do at the end, and the question they answer.

### Naming the mechanism

- The name should be ownable, short, and sayable. The brand's own name plus a plain noun works.
- Attach a physical image and spell it out once: "a relay is what carries the customer to your door".
- Three pillars with parallel wording. Each one answers "what happens, and why does that help me?"
- Named once clearly at the end of part 2, then repeated inside the FAQ answers.

## Writing each part

**Hook.** First sentence selects the viewer by identity. Second opens the question. No greeting, no name, no warm-up.

**Pain.** Their words, in quotation marks. Then one external, dated fact. No sourced fact? Use the verbatims alone and list the gap when you deliver. Do not exaggerate. An accurate description of their week is more painful than any adjective.

**Respect the old way.** They built their business with it. Say so before you say why it stopped being enough: "Your reputation built your business. Keep it. It does not let you choose." Condescension loses the room.

**The shared flaw.** Do not attack methods one by one. Show that all of them have the same defect, and let that single sentence do the work.

**Why newcomers win.** Offer the viewer an example they can already picture: "you probably know one". No invented case. Explain what those newcomers do, in three verbs.

**FAQ, stage by stage.**

- *What they think:* in quotation marks, as they would say it. Do not soften it.
- *Why it is wrong:* one short sentence that turns the objection around. Often it agrees with the feeling and changes the frame: "You do not have time to run ads. Precisely, you will not run them."
- *What is right:* concrete, tied to the mechanism, physical nouns.
- *Proof:* one fact, with the context visible.

**The offer loop.** State the duration, list what is included, say what stays theirs, and say what they must do. Stating the client's effort honestly raises belief.

**Price.** Reframe first, against the cost of doing nothing. Separate amounts that go to different places. Never invent a number: if it is not set, say it is given in writing before any commitment.

**Guarantee.** Name the condition, name what is excluded, say where it is written. Put legal caveats in the production note and get real guarantees reviewed for the regulated profession or market concerned. A clause you say is in the contract must be in the contract.

**Urgency.** Only if true. A deadline that resets is a lie, and it will be noticed.

**CTA.** The smallest possible next step, and a question that makes the next conversation theirs.

**Proof salad.** Variety matters: figures, named companies, quotes, totals. Quotes are exact. Always name the sector.

## When the material is thin

A thin brief makes a shorter video, so choose the short format. If you stay long, lengthen only with what you already know: the "why that helps me" under each pillar, the offer and guarantee stages, dense slides split in two. Never add a fact, client, number or example to reach a word count. A figure you cannot source and a mechanism detail the owner never gave you are the same mistake.

## Sentence rules

Written for the ear, delivered by a person, read off a screen.

- One idea per slide. 27 spoken words at most, 14 on average.
- Short declarative sentences. A long one earns its place by being followed by a short one.
- Concrete nouns, things one can see or count. Replace every abstraction you find.
- Spell numbers in `text` the way they are spoken. Use digits in `key` and in `stats`.
- Accent color on the phrase to remember, not on the longest words, and not everywhere.
- Second person. Formal or informal to match the market, and hold it.
- Define any word that means something else in the viewer's trade at first mention.
- One touch of self-deprecation at most, in the proof block of the intro. It buys trust.
- Precise about who does what. If the client makes the call, never write "we call back".
- Use the viewer's vocabulary. Remove seller vocabulary such as conversion, pipeline, positioning.

## What to remove

- "It is not X, it is Y", more than twice in the whole script.
- Triplets that add nothing.
- Exclamation marks, emoji, long dashes.
- Hype without proof: unbelievable, revolutionary, game changer.
- Hedges on screen. If a claim needs a caveat, either make the claim narrower or put the caveat in the note.
- Anything that describes the presentation instead of the subject: "as this diagram shows".

## Truth rules

- No invented figure, client, testimonial, quote or study.
- Quotes are exact. If you shorten one for speech, say so in the note.
- Results from other sectors are allowed if the context line says so.
- Illustrative interfaces carry the tag "Example".
- If you have no result in the viewer's trade, say so plainly and make it the reason you carry the risk.
- Claims that regulated professions, health, finance or guarantees touch get a production note and human review before broadcast.

## Review pass

Do all of this before delivering, on the actual script.

1. **Read it aloud, start to finish.** Anything you stumble on gets rewritten. Time it, and set `wpm` from the measurement.
2. **Trace every claim.** Each fact points to the brief or to the proof inventory, or it goes.
3. **The skeptical buyer.** Read as someone who has been burned before. Mark each place you would roll your eyes, and fix those.
4. **The "so what" test.** Under each feature, is the benefit audible?
5. **The screen-only test.** In screen mode, do the `key` lines alone tell the story?
6. **Cut 10%.** There is always 10%.
7. **Language pass.** Have a native reader's ear on it. French: non-breaking punctuation is automatic, so check register and anglicisms.

## Bad to good

| Weak | Why | Better |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "We help businesses grow thanks to a unique method." | No result, no image, no one in particular | "You will sign one to three good contracts in ninety days." |
| "We are just starting out, so we cannot guarantee anything." | Hedge on screen | "You would be among the first. That is why the risk is ours." |
| "Our clients get amazing results!" | Hype, no figure, exclamation | "A family-run garage: forty new requests in two months." |
| "It is not about marketing, it is about strategy." | Empty contrast | "Your next client is not looking for you yet. They are planning a renovation." |
| "Click here or send us a message or follow us." | Three actions | "Click the button under this video and book your call." |
