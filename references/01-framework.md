# The five-part framework

Structure from Alex Hormozi's 5-part VSL framework, shown in a [short public video](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DfaGTlsoI7m). What follows is our working version, applied slide by slide.

Every VSL uses these five parts, in this order, for any market or length. Other copywriting frameworks fill the parts. They never replace one.

| # | Part | Job | Share of running time | In a 104-slide deck |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Intro | Earn the next ten minutes | 10 to 17% | 18 slides |
| 2 | Old way vs new way | Show why your approach is different, and name it | 15 to 22% | 20 slides |
| 3 | FAQ loops | Remove every remaining objection | 40 to 55% | 53 slides |
| 4 | Small CTA | One easy action and one personal question | 2 to 6% | 3 slides |
| 5 | Proof salad | Nothing but proof until the end | 10 to 25% | 10 slides |

## 1. Intro

Six blocks. `hook` always comes first. The others may swap when one lands harder, for instance proof before promise.

| Block | What it does | Pattern |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `hook` | Names who this is for, then opens a question they cannot leave unanswered | "If you run a [business], one question." then the question |
| `promise` | States the precise result | [result] in [delay], without [pain], even if [objection] |
| `pain` | Describes today in their own words | Two or three verbatims, then one hard fact about the market |
| `proof` | Makes the promise believable at once, one to three items | Who you are in one line, then the strongest figures |
| `plan` | Says where the video is going | One sentence |
| `picture` | Makes them see life after the result | One concrete moment: a day, a desk, a phone |

## 2. Old way vs new way

Choose one angle: old way against new way, them against us, or the hidden problem. Four blocks:

| Block | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| `old` | Lists what the market has always done. Respectfully: those methods built their business |
| `why` | Shows the one flaw all of them share, then the turning point: what the buyer is actually doing now |
| `shift` (optional) | Explains who is already winning and why time no longer protects the incumbents |
| `new` | Names the mechanism and walks through its pillars. Always the last block |

No proof in this part. It explains, and the FAQ proves. The transition out is one short line that hands over to the questions.

## 3. FAQ loops

The viewer now thinks "that sounds good, but...". Say the "but" for them, then close it in four stages:

| Stage | Content | Usually |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. What they think | The objection in the words they would use | 1 slide, often a verbatim |
| 2. Why it is wrong | What that belief does not see | 1 slide |
| 3. What is right | The truth, tied to the named mechanism. Offer details live here | 1 to 5 slides |
| 4. Proof | A figure or a client that demonstrates it | 1 slide |

Order the loops the way the questions naturally arrive:

1. Has this been done for someone like me?
2. Will it bring the wrong kind of customers?
3. Is it beneath me, is it allowed, must I perform?
4. I already tried something like this.
5. I do not have time.
6. Is it too soon, or too short?
7. What exactly do I get? (`kind='offer'`)
8. How much does it cost? (`kind='price'`)
9. What if it does not work? (`kind='guarantee'`)
10. Is the free thing a disguised sales call?

Long format: 6 to 10 loops. Short format: 3 to 5. Offer, price and guarantee are mandatory in both. The mechanism is named 4 to 6 times across the loops (2 to 4 in short format).

## 4. Small CTA

One action, small and easy. One personalization question the person answers as they act, such as how many clients they have, the size of the project, the budget. It makes the next conversation personal and shows they watched. The question must be exactly the one in `brand.json`, and it must exist in the real form.

No alternative action, ever. No second offer.

## 5. Proof salad

Everything after the CTA is proof: figures, named clients, testimonials, in volume and variety. Whoever is still watching is hesitating, and only more proof moves them. No new argument. The button stays on screen. The last slide repeats the single action in one sentence.

## Skeleton

```text
1. Intro: hook · promise · pain · proof (1 to 3) · plan · picture
2. Old way vs new way: old · why · shift · new (names the mechanism)
3. FAQ
   Q1 "..." : think / wrong / right / proof
   Q7 "What exactly do I get?" : think / wrong / right (offer, stack) / proof
   Q8 "How much?" : think / wrong / right (price, anchor) / proof
   Q9 "What if it fails?" : think / wrong / right (guarantee) / proof
4. CTA: action · personalization question
5. Proof: salad, then one line that repeats the action
```

## Where the classic blocks go

- Hook, who it is for and who it is not, authority: part 1.
- Starting point, what they tried, real problem, common enemy, realization, name of the method: part 2.
- Objections, benefits, offer and value stack, price, guarantee, urgency: part 3.
- Call to action: part 4.
- Results and testimonials: part 1 (one to three), the end of each loop, part 5.
